How it works

Three steps. No model in the hot path.

The decision about which frames matter is made by a signal that costs 1–2 ms per frame on CPU — cheaper than the call it avoids. That was the whole point: an earlier version ran a detector costing 120 ms to skip a 5 ms encode, and lost.

STEP 01

Measure change

A cheap signal runs over a 160×90 downscale — appearance, structure and motion with camera movement cancelled out. A panning camera moves every pixel while nothing happens; that gets filtered.

STEP 02

Spend the budget

The cumulative-change curve is cut into equal-area segments and one call is spent per segment. Static stretches collapse to a single call; busy stretches earn more. A hard trigger guarantees a sudden cut is never averaged away.

STEP 03

Call your model

You get frame indices. Send them to GPT-4o, Gemini, Claude, CLIP — whatever you already use. We never touch your model, your prompts or your keys.

Where it fits

Drops in front of whatever you already run.

frameroute sits between your video and your model. Nothing downstream changes — same model, same prompts, same output schema, fewer calls.

🐍

Python SDK

Three lines in front of your existing loop. The most common integration.

# before: you embedded every frame
from frameroute import FrameRouter, analyze_video

track = analyze_video("lecture.mp4")
for pick in FrameRouter().select(track, budget_per_hour=240).picks:
    answer = your_vlm(frame_at(pick.idx))   # your model, untouched
🌐

REST API

Any language. Post a video URL, get back the frames worth spending on.

curl -X POST https://api.frameroute.dev/v1/route \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer $FR_KEY" \
  -d '{"url":"https://...lecture.mp4","budget_per_hour":240}'

# → {"picks":[0,143,512,...], "calls_saved":3421}

docker run -v /media:/media frameroute/router \
  route /media/lecture.mp4 --budget-per-hour 240

At 67–74% fewer calls, retrieval was better than sending every frame — because a sparse index spreads its results across the timeline instead of clustering on five near-identical frames from one moment.

What we don’t claim

Our selection algorithm did not beat plain uniform sampling. At identical call counts it won one lecture by 25% and lost the other by 13%. The saving comes from sending fewer frames — not from our picker being cleverer than the ten lines you’d write yourself.

We also withdrew two earlier quality claims after better measurement contradicted them, and our evidence is two lectures, not a published benchmark. Continuous-motion footage — traffic, crowds, fast sport — is where routing helps least.

We publish this because the number is the product. A figure that collapses under your scrutiny would be worth less than nothing to both of us.
